CYBERSECURITY LESSON

Two-Factor Authentication 🔐

Learn how 2FA adds an extra layer of protection to your online accounts.

🔐 What is Two-Factor Authentication?

Two-factor authentication, commonly called 2FA, is a security method that requires two different types of verification before allowing access to an account.

Even if someone discovers your password, a second verification factor can help prevent unauthorized access.

🧩 The Three Authentication Factors

Authentication factors generally fall into three categories:

  • Something you know: A password or PIN.
  • Something you have: A security key, phone, or authentication device.
  • Something you are: A biometric characteristic such as a fingerprint or face recognition.

📱 Examples of 2FA

  • Authentication apps that generate temporary codes.
  • Security keys.
  • One-time verification codes.
  • Biometric verification combined with another factor.

🛡️ Why is 2FA Important?

Passwords can sometimes be stolen through phishing, data breaches, malware, or password reuse.

2FA provides an additional security layer, making it harder for an attacker to access an account using only a stolen password.

⭐ 2FA Best Practices

  1. Enable 2FA on important accounts whenever available.
  2. Prefer strong authentication methods such as security keys or authenticator applications when appropriate.
  3. Never share verification codes with other people.
  4. Keep recovery methods secure.
  5. Don't approve unexpected authentication requests.

🚨 Important Warning

A legitimate organization should not ask you to give them your one-time authentication code.

If someone calls or messages you asking for a verification code, stop and independently verify who they are.

💡 Remember

Your password is only one layer of security. Adding another authentication factor can significantly improve account protection.
