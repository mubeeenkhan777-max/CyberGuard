CYBERSECURITY LESSON

Social Engineering 🎭

Learn how attackers manipulate people into making unsafe decisions or revealing information.

🎭 What is Social Engineering?

Social engineering is the use of psychological manipulation or deception to influence people into revealing information or performing actions that may compromise security.

Instead of attacking a computer directly, an attacker may try to convince a person to make a mistake.

🎯 Common Social Engineering Techniques

  • Pretexting: Creating a believable story or identity to obtain information.
  • Impersonation: Pretending to be a trusted person or organization.
  • Baiting: Offering something attractive to encourage an unsafe action.
  • Quid pro quo: Offering a supposed benefit in exchange for information or an action.
  • Tailgating: Gaining physical access by following an authorized person into a restricted area.

🧠 Psychological Tricks

Social engineering attacks often rely on predictable human reactions.

  • Urgency: "You must do this immediately."
  • Fear: "Your account will be closed."
  • Authority: Pretending to be a manager, bank employee, government official, or IT administrator.
  • Curiosity: Encouraging someone to open something because it appears interesting.
  • Trust: Using familiar names, logos, or relationships to appear legitimate.

🚨 Warning Signs

  • Someone unexpectedly asks for confidential information.
  • Someone pressures you to act immediately.
  • Someone asks you to bypass normal procedures.
  • Someone asks for passwords, PINs, or verification codes.
  • The request seems unusual even though the person appears trustworthy.
  • You are discouraged from verifying the request.

🛡️ How to Protect Yourself

  1. Stop and think before responding.
  2. Verify unexpected requests independently.
  3. Never share passwords or authentication codes.
  4. Follow established security procedures.
  5. Don't allow urgency or fear to make the decision for you.
  6. Report suspicious requests to the appropriate person or organization.

⭐ Remember

If someone pressures you to reveal sensitive information or bypass normal security procedures, stop and verify the request through a trusted channel.
